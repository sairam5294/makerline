# Competitor product teardown (Standard: top 1–2 · Deep: top 3)

Picture one leader's whole product from public material, name where it differs from ours, and pick out the lessons. Each teardown is a `## Teardown — <competitor>` section in COMPETITORS.md (≤ 400 words each at Standard). It sits on top of the four-ring method, not in place of it.

## Ground rules

- **Learn from them; don't clone them.** Public pages only, or a free trial the owner opens in their own name. No getting past logins, paywalls or robots rules; no leaked files.
- Take none of their code, text, screenshots, design assets or brand. "Copy" means meeting the same user expectation with our own build. Mark anything that seems patented or tied to their brand.
- A blocked or paid source → "not checked", with no workaround.

## Where to look (best yield first, within the depth's page-read budget)

| Source | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| Index of help or docs articles | The fullest feature list: each "how to" title is one capability, so one page read covers dozens |
| Pricing page | Packaging: which tier holds which feature, limits, trial |
| Changelog, release notes, app-store "What's new" | Recent releases and the pace of shipping |
| Reviews (likes and dislikes) | What users prize and where it fails (reuse the R-ids from Leader profiles) |
| API docs, integrations marketplace | Platform depth, who builds on it, which partners they chase |
| Job ads (careers page, LinkedIn) | Their next build: "Signal: likely building <X> in 6–12 months" |
| Status page, security or trust page, terms | Uptime record, certifications, where data lives, lock-in terms |
| Old pricing pages (web archive) | Direction: new tiers, tighter limits, higher prices |
| Community (forum, subreddit, Discord), talks by their engineers | Needs users say aren't met; how it works under the hood |
| Demo videos, webinars | Actual screens and flows (describe in words; never copy frames) |
| Public sandbox or free trial | Hands-on proof, on the owner's own sign-up only; no real data, no contacting staff |

**Keep only rows that change a decision** (what to build, how to position, what to test); each ends "→ so we…".

**Dated moves** (releases, pricing, hiring, funding, rules) also go to the industry's Dated signals (`portfolio/references/industry-file.md` in the full MakerLine kit) for the next product in this industry.

## What to write (tables, not prose)

**A. Capability map.** Columns: Capability | Them (Yes / Partly / No, depth Basic / Solid / Advanced) | This idea | Tier it sits in | Confidence | R-id. Confidence is **Confirmed** (we saw it in the product, docs or a video), **Claimed** (only marketing says so) or **Inferred**.

**B. What they do differently**, beyond features: onboarding (self-serve or demo), pricing model, target user, integrations, AI features, support, distribution. Row: the move · what it means for our user (example) · R-id.

**C. Worth learning from.** One row per item, each with a verdict:
- **Copy**: table stakes. Users expect it and leave without it.
- **Adapt**: a good idea we rebuild to fit our wedge better.
- **Leapfrog**: they point the way and we go one step further (run the idea-champion's novelty test).
- **Ignore**: wrong for the goal, wedge or stage. Give the reason.

**D. Where we're already ahead.** Things to protect and to use in the positioning statement.

Copy, Adapt and Leapfrog rows go into the strengthening plan, later into `docs/product/IDEAS.md` with Source "Teardown: <competitor>". Record the teardown date; redo it only after a logged major release, showing just the changes.
