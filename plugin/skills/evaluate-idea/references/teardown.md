# Competitor product teardown (Standard: top 1–2 · Deep: top 3)

Map a leader's whole product from what is public, then say plainly what they do differently and what is worth learning from. Adds to COMPETITORS.md (a `## Teardown — <competitor>` section, ≤ 400 words each at Standard); it doesn't replace the four-ring method.

**Learn, don't clone.** Use only public pages, or a free trial the owner signs up for themselves. Never bypass a login, paywall or robots rule, never use leaked material, and never copy their code, wording, screenshots, design assets or brand. "Copy" means adopting the customer expectation, rebuilt your own way. Flag anything that looks patented or uniquely branded.

## 1. Sources, richest first (inside the depth's page-read budget)

| Source | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| Help center / docs article index | The most complete feature list: every "how do I…" title is a capability. One page read covers dozens. |
| Pricing page | Packaging: which feature sits in which tier, limits, trial |
| Changelog / release notes / app-store "What's new" | What shipped recently and how fast they move |
| Reviews ("what I like / dislike") | What users value and where it breaks (already mined in Leader profiles — reuse the R-ids) |
| API docs, integrations, job ads, status page | Platform depth, what they're building next, reliability |

A blocked or paid source → "not checked", no workaround.

## 2. Output (tables, not prose)

**A. Capability map** — Capability | Them (Yes / Partly / No, depth Basic / Solid / Advanced) | This idea | Tier it sits in | Confidence: **Confirmed** (seen in product, docs or video) / **Claimed** (marketing only) / **Inferred** | R-id.

**B. What they do differently** (beyond features): onboarding (self-serve vs demo), pricing model, who they target, integrations, AI features, support, distribution. Row: what they do · why it matters to our user (example) · R-id.

**C. Worth learning from** — one row per item with a verdict:
- **Copy** = table stakes; users expect it and its absence loses them.
- **Adapt** = good idea; do it our way, better fitted to our wedge.
- **Leapfrog** = they show the direction; go a step beyond (run the idea-champion's novelty test).
- **Ignore** = doesn't fit the goal, wedge or stage — say why.

**D. Where we're already ahead** — to protect, and to use in the positioning statement.

Copy / Adapt / Leapfrog rows feed the strengthening plan (and later `docs/product/IDEAS.md` with Source "Teardown: <competitor>"). Save the teardown date; re-run only when a major release is logged, and show only what changed.
