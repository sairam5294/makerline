# Operations scan: the company behind the product (Standard and Deep)

The product's users are one audience. The team that sells to, onboards, supports and invoices them is another. Time saved there cuts the client's or employer's costs directly, often faster than a feature. Read-only; nothing is changed.

## 1. Gather evidence

**Already in reach:**

| Source | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Admin screens and code | customer setup steps, the invoicing flow, support's tools. Setup typed in by hand is an automation candidate |
| Backlog and tracker | work for internal teams: help centre, self-serve sign-up, billing automation, reporting |
| Ticket history | volume, top themes, first-reply time, repeat problems (`research-synthesizer` counts them) |
| Analytics | onboarding steps and days to first value; where customers get stuck and contact support |
| Public | missing help topics, job ads (support roles = support load), reviews about support or onboarding |
| Industry File | how the leaders handle support, onboarding and sales (self-serve, AI on the front line) |

**Ask for the rest, in one short request through the right person:** a ticket export or summary · onboarding steps and duration · lead sources and sales-cycle length · how invoices go out and late payers are chased · headcount by function · analytics access · 30 minutes with the ops or support lead. Pitch it as time back for their people. If the account-health note says the relationship is shaky, agree timing with the account owner first: questions about internal work can feel like an audit.

## 2. Map the work

Per team, each workflow with owner, frequency, steps, time and tools. Illustrative: "Support: 2 people, about 60 tickets a month, 4 in 10 are how-to, first reply in about 6 hours."

## 3. Look for fixes

Run each workflow through `../../ai-feature/references/ai-patterns.md`, writing the plain fix first: a clearer help article, a shorter setup screen, a report that sends itself. Note per candidate: hours freed a month (people × hours, labelled estimate) · money saved or earned · data needed and whether they hold it · who signs off on the AI's output · what staff would do with the time · effort · already planned?

Common finds in a software business (illustrative): suggested replies and auto-routing for tickets · importing a new customer's spreadsheets at setup · renewal-risk flags for account managers · drafted chasers for overdue invoices · call notes turned into CRM entries · a one-page monthly digest for leaders.

## 4. Choose pilots

Take the top three on hours freed × data on hand × low risk. Run each for 2–4 weeks with a single team, measured before and after: say, the share of tickets closed with no human reply, or days from signed contract to live account. Add each to `ai-risk-register.md`.

## Output

A "Client operations" section in AUDIT.md (`Team | How it works now | Fix (AI or not) | Hours freed / month | Data | Who approves | Effort | Already planned?`), the request list, and the top items scored beside product ideas in the review. In anything the client reads, call these "time your team could get back", never a verdict on how the business is run. Headcount and pay stay internal.
