# Operations scan: the company behind the product (Standard and Deep)

The product's users are one audience; the team that supports, onboards, sells and bills them is another. Savings here hit the client's or employer's costs directly and are often quicker wins than features. Read-only; nothing is changed.

## 1. What you can see without asking

| Source | Look for |
|---|---|
| Admin screens and code | how a customer is set up, how billing runs, what support staff use (hand-entered setup = an automation candidate) |
| Backlog and tracker | items for internal teams: help centre, self sign-up, billing automation, reporting |
| Tickets and support exports | volume, top themes, time to first reply, repeat issues (`research-synthesizer` counts them) |
| Analytics | onboarding steps and days to first value; where customers stall and then ask for help |
| Public | the help centre's gaps, job ads (hiring support = support load), reviews about support or onboarding |
| Industry Brain | how leaders run support, onboarding and sales (self-serve, AI first line) |

## 2. What to ask for (one short request through the right person)

Support ticket export or summary · onboarding steps and days · how leads arrive and how long deals take · invoicing and late-payment process · team size per function · analytics access · a 30-minute walkthrough with the ops or support lead. Frame it as finding time for their teams. If the relationship is fragile (account-health note), check the timing with the account owner first; questions about internal operations can feel like an inspection.

## 3. Map each function's workflows

Function · workflow · who · how often · steps · time · tools. Example (illustrative): "Support: ~60 tickets/month, 40% how-to, 2 people, 6 h to first reply."

## 4. Apply the seven AI patterns, and the non-AI fix

Use `../../ai-feature/references/ai-patterns.md` on each workflow; always write the non-AI alternative (a better help article, a simpler setup screen, an automatic report) first. Record per opportunity: hours saved per month (people × hours, labelled estimate) · money saved or earned · data needed and whether they already have it · who approves what the AI does · what staff would do with the time · effort · already planned?

Typical findings for a software company: AI first-line support answering how-to questions from the help centre · guided or automated customer setup from their old data · early-warning outreach to struggling customers · self sign-up for small customers · automatic payment reminders · a weekly leadership summary from product and support data.

## 5. Pick and prove

Top 3 by hours saved × data available × low risk → a 2–4 week pilot with one team and a before/after measure (tickets answered without a person, setup days per customer). Each gets an AI risk register row (`ai-risk-register.md`).

## Output

A "Client operations" section in AUDIT.md (`Function | Workflow today | AI or non-AI fix | Hours saved / month | Data needed | Approval step | Effort | Planned?`), the request list, and the top items scored beside product ideas in the review. In client documents, present them as "ways to save your team time", never as a judgement of how they run the business. Staff numbers and salaries stay internal.
